The Numbers

How Common Are HPV and Cervical Cancer?

Numbers help us see how big a health problem is. Here is what the data say about HPV and cervical cancer, which is cancer of the cervix (the lower part of the uterus, or womb, that opens into the vagina).

Two words to know

Prevalence

Prevalence = how many people have it right now.

Incidence

Incidence = how many new cases happen in a period of time.

Worldwide

Rank among cancers in women

5th

Cervical cancer is the fifth most common cancer in women worldwide (World Health Organization [WHO], 2026).

New cases (incidence)

604,000

About 604,000 new cases of cervical cancer were estimated in 2024 (WHO, 2026).

Deaths

280,000

About 280,000 deaths from cervical cancer occurred in 2024 (WHO, 2026).

United States

HPV prevalence

42 million+

Prevalence: more than 42 million Americans are infected with types of HPV known to cause disease (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention [CDC], 2024a).

HPV incidence

13 million

HPV incidence: about 13 million Americans, including teens, become infected each year (CDC, 2024a).

Cervical cancer incidence

13,490

Cervical cancer incidence: about 13,490 new cases of invasive cervical cancer (cancer that has grown from the surface of the cervix into deeper tissue) are expected in 2026 (American Cancer Society [ACS], 2026).

Deaths

4,200

Deaths: about 4,200 women are expected to die from cervical cancer in 2026 (ACS, 2026).

Caused by HPV

9 in 10+

More than 9 out of 10 cervical cancer cases are caused by HPV (CDC, 2025a). Each year, about 10,800 women are diagnosed with cervical cancer caused by HPV, and nearly 200,000 are diagnosed with cervical precancer (cell changes that could turn into cancer if not treated) (CDC, 2025a).

All HPV cancers

36,000

HPV causes about 36,000 cases of cancer in men and women each year (CDC, 2024a).

Hamilton County, Ohio

Stable

trend over time

From 2019 to 2023, Hamilton County averaged 36 new cervical cancer cases per year, a rate of 8.3 cases per 100,000 females, and the trend was stable (National Cancer Institute & Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, n.d.).

A rate lets us compare places with different numbers of people. A stable trend means the rate did not clearly go up or down.

11%

Good news

Cervical cancer rates have dropped by 11% each year among women ages 20–24, probably an early sign that HPV vaccination is working (ACS, 2026).

Quick Takeaway

HPV is very common. It causes more than 9 out of 10 cervical cancer cases (CDC, 2025a). The good news: cervical cancer rates are falling fast among women ages 20–24, probably an early sign that the vaccine is working (ACS, 2026).
