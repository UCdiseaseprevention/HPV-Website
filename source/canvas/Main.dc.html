What Is HPV?

The basics, in plain language.

Very common

HPV (human papillomavirus) is a very common virus. A virus is a tiny germ that can make you sick. Nearly everyone who is not vaccinated will get HPV at some point in their lives (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention [CDC], 2024a).

How it spreads

It spreads through intimate skin-to-skin contact, including vaginal, anal, or oral sex, even when the person has no signs or symptoms (CDC, 2024a).

Most clear up

About 9 out of 10 HPV infections go away on their own within 2 years (CDC, 2024a).

Some cause cancer

Infections that last longer can cause cancers of the cervix (the lower part of the uterus, or womb, that opens into the vagina), vagina, vulva (the outer genital area), penis, anus, and back of the throat (CDC, 2024a).

Cancer is slow

Cancer usually takes years, even decades, to develop after a person gets HPV (CDC, 2025a).

From HPV to Cancer and How to Stop It

Cervical cancer grows slowly, in steps. That gives you two chances to stop it.

Quick Takeaway

HPV is very common, and about 9 out of 10 infections go away on their own (CDC, 2024a). Cervical cancer takes years to develop, so you have time to act (CDC, 2025a). Your two main tools are the HPV vaccine and regular screening.
